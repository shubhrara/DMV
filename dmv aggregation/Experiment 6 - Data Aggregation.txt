Title: Analyzing Sales Performance by Region in a Retail Company
Dataset: Global Superstore Dataset.csv

============================================================
1. WHAT THIS PRACTICAL DOES
============================================================

This practical analyses retail sales using the Global Superstore dataset. It groups sales by region, calculates total sales for each region, identifies the highest-performing region, groups sales by region and product category, and compares the results using graphs.

The notebook uses:
- Pandas: reading, grouping and analysing tabular data.
- Matplotlib: displaying graphs.

Dataset facts:
- 9,800 rows and 17 columns.
- Important columns used: Region, Category and Sales.
- The dataset does NOT contain a quantity-sold column. Therefore, this practical analyses sales value only. It does not calculate units sold.

Keep these files in the same folder:
1. DMV_Experiment_6_Data_Aggregation.ipynb
2. Global Superstore Dataset.csv

Run the notebook cells from top to bottom.

5. CALCULATE TOTAL SALES FOR EACH REGION – TASK 4, CELL 8
============================================================

Code:
region_sales = df.groupby("Region")["Sales"].sum().sort_values(ascending=False)
print(region_sales)

Break it into steps:

1. df.groupby("Region")
   Groups all rows that have the same Region value. For example, all West records form one group, all East records form another, and so on.

2. ["Sales"]
   Selects the Sales column from those groups. We only want to calculate sales totals.

3. .sum()
   Adds the Sales values within each region.

4. .sort_values(ascending=False)
   Sorts the resulting regional totals from highest to lowest.
   - ascending=True means smallest to largest.
   - ascending=False means largest to smallest.

5. region_sales =
   Stores the final sorted result in the variable region_sales.

6. print(region_sales)
   Displays the total sales for each region.

Actual result for the supplied dataset (rounded to two decimal places):

Region       Total Sales
West         710,219.68
East         669,518.73
Central      492,646.91
South        389,151.46

What is aggregation?
Aggregation means combining multiple records into a summary value. Here, many individual sales transactions are combined into one total for each region.

Why group by Region?
The aim is to compare sales performance across geographical regions. Grouping collects transactions belonging to the same region before calculating their total.

Viva:
Q. What is groupby()?
A. A Pandas operation that divides rows into groups based on common values in one or more columns.

Q. What is aggregation?
A. Summarising multiple records using a function such as sum, mean, count, minimum or maximum.

Q. What does sum() do here?
A. Adds all Sales values belonging to each region.

Q. What does sort_values() do?
A. Sorts values in ascending or descending order.

Q. What does ascending=False mean?
A. Sort from highest to lowest.

Q. What happens if ascending=True is used?
A. The region with the smallest sales total appears first.

Q. Can groupby() use more than one column?
A. Yes. For example, groupby(["Region", "Category"]) creates groups based on both columns.

8. CALCULATE SALES BY REGION AND PRODUCT CATEGORY – TASK 7, CELL 14
============================================================

Code:
region_category_sales = df.groupby(["Region", "Category"])["Sales"].sum().unstack()
print(region_category_sales)

Break it into steps:

1. df.groupby(["Region", "Category"])
   Groups records using two columns together.
   For example, one group contains West-Furniture sales, another contains West-Technology sales, and so on.

2. ["Sales"]
   Selects the Sales column for calculation.

3. .sum()
   Adds the sales values inside every Region-Category group.

4. .unstack()
   Reshapes the grouped result. It moves the inner grouping level (Category) from the row index into separate columns.

5. region_category_sales =
   Stores the resulting table.

6. print(...)
   Displays the table.

Result for the supplied dataset (rounded):

Region     Furniture     Office Supplies     Technology
Central    160,317.46    163,590.24           168,739.21
East       206,461.39    199,940.81           263,116.53
South      116,531.48    124,424.77           148,195.21
West       245,348.25    217,466.51           247,404.93

What does unstack() do?
Before unstacking, the result has a hierarchical index containing Region and Category. unstack() pivots the inner index level, Category, into columns. This makes it easier to compare product categories side by side for each region.

Viva:
Q. Why group by two columns?
A. To calculate sales separately for every combination of region and product category.

Q. What does unstack() do?
A. Moves an inner index level into columns, reshaping the result into a table.

Q. What is a hierarchical or MultiIndex?
A. An index with more than one level. Here, the grouped result uses Region and Category as index levels before unstacking.

Q. What does the final table show?
A. Each row represents a region, each column represents a product category, and each cell contains the total sales for that combination.

Explanation:

- title gives the graph a heading.
- plt.xlabel("Region") labels the horizontal axis.
- plt.ylabel("Sales") labels the vertical axis.
- plt.xticks(rotation=0) keeps region names horizontal.
- plt.legend(title="Product Category") displays the legend and gives it a title.
- plt.tight_layout() adjusts spacing.
- plt.show() displays the graph.

How to read it:
For every region, compare the three bars: Furniture, Office Supplies and Technology. Their heights show the sales amounts for those categories.

PART B – STACKED BAR CHART

Code:
region_category_sales.plot(kind="bar", stacked=True, title="Stacked Sales by Region and Product Category")
plt.xlabel("Region")
plt.ylabel("Sales")
plt.xticks(rotation=0)
plt.legend(title="Product Category")
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

Explanation:
- kind="bar" selects a bar chart.
- stacked=True places the category bars on top of one another for each region instead of side by side.
- The remaining commands label the axes, display the legend, adjust spacing and show the graph.

How to read it:
Each complete bar represents the combined sales of all three product categories for one region. The coloured sections show how much each category contributes to that regional total.

Difference between grouped and stacked bar charts:
- Grouped bar chart: category bars are side by side, making direct category comparisons easier.
- Stacked bar chart: category values are placed on top of each other, making the total and each category's contribution visible.

Viva:
Q. What is a grouped bar chart?
A. A chart in which bars for different categories appear side by side within each group.

Q. What is a stacked bar chart?
A. A chart in which category values are stacked on top of one another to show the total and its components.

Q. What does stacked=True do?
A. Stacks the category bars instead of displaying them side by side.

Q. What does legend() do?
A. Displays the labels that identify the plotted categories.

Q. Which chart is better for comparing individual categories?
A. A grouped bar chart is usually easier for direct side-by-side comparisons.

Q. Which chart helps show the total and its composition?
A. A stacked bar chart.
